\begin{abstract}
Hate speech detection is predominantly formulated as an isolated, disjoint classification task, ignoring intrinsic logical dependencies between hate type, target, and severity. In low-resource languages like Bengali, frontier Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle to maintain this structural consistency during generation, frequently outputting impossible contradictions (e.g., predicting that a text is non-hateful, while simultaneously labeling the target as a community and the severity as severe). In this paper, we propose a Consistency-Constrained Multi-Task Learning (MTL) architecture based on BanglaBERT that jointly predicts Hate Type (5 classes), Target (5 classes), and Severity (3 classes). To mathematically bound the loss surface and eliminate contradictions, we introduce a novel, fully differentiable Soft Consistency Loss. Furthermore, to provide transparent interpretability, our model incorporates a lightweight LSTM-based generative decoder trained on human-verified rationales, generating natural language explanations for its predictions. Evaluated on a strictly held-out test set of 3,553 Bengali comments (drawn from a corpus of 35,530 samples), our 110M parameter model reduces the Consistency Violation Rate (CVR) from 9.46\% (336 violations) in the unconstrained baseline to 0.03\% (just 1 violation), while improving Average Macro F1 from 0.540 to 0.558. In comparative benchmarking against dedicated Bengali LLMs---including TigerLLM-1B, TituLLM-1B, and BongLLaMA-3B---our model achieves a +68.8\% relative improvement in Macro F1 over the best LLM baseline (0.558 vs. 0.330), operates $\sim$390$\times$ faster (0.015s vs. 5.85s per sample), and guarantees 100\% deterministic output parsing.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Online social platforms in Bangladesh and the broader Bengali-speaking world have witnessed an alarming surge in hate speech, targeted harassment, and toxic discourse \cite{islam2021bangla}. Automated detection of such content is vital for digital safety; however, Bengali presents formidable natural language processing (NLP) challenges due to rich inflectional morphology, widespread informal phonetic transliteration (Banglish), extensive code-mixing, and subtle socio-cultural nuances \cite{romero2019bengali}.

Conventional research predominantly frames hate speech detection as an isolated, single-task classification problem (binary toxic vs. non-toxic, or categorical hate type) \cite{romero2021hate}. While functional for basic filtering, this disjoint formulation ignores the multi-dimensional anatomy of hate speech: identifying \textit{what} category of hate is present (Type), \textit{who} is being attacked (Target), and \textit{how threatening} the expression is (Severity). Crucially, these dimensions are governed by strict hierarchical rules: if a comment contains no hate speech ($\text{Type} = \text{None}$), it logically cannot target an entity ($\text{Target} = \text{None}$) nor inflict harm ($\text{Severity} = \text{Little to None}$). Unconstrained multi-task models and generative Large Language Models (LLMs) routinely violate this semantic hierarchy, producing glaring hallucinations and contradictory outputs.

Recently, generative Large Language Models such as LLaMA \cite{touvron2023llama}, Gemini \cite{team2023gemini}, and region-specific models like TigerLLM \cite{nishat2025tigerllm} have demonstrated impressive zero-shot reasoning. However, as our empirical experiments demonstrate, LLMs face severe operational bottlenecks in low-resource settings:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Structural Hallucinations:} Unconstrained LLMs frequently output incompatible attribute combinations (e.g., TigerLLM produces 47 consistency violations, while BongLLaMA exhibits catastrophic mode collapse with 100\% violations under few-shot prompting).
    \item \textbf{Format Fragility:} Complex JSON prompt compliance is highly brittle; without extensive in-context demonstrations, models like TituLLM and BongLLaMA suffer parse failure rates between 78.5\% and 100\%.
    \item \textbf{High Latency \& Resource Overhead:} Operating 1B--3B parameter models incurs latencies between 1.28s and 5.85s per comment, rendering them computationally prohibitive for real-time edge moderation.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these fundamental limitations, we present a unified, efficient, and interpretable framework for Bengali hate speech detection. Our main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Consistency-Constrained Multi-Task Architecture:} We formulate multi-aspect Bengali hate speech detection as a joint optimization problem over BanglaBERT \cite{bhattacharjee2022banglabert}, simultaneously predicting Hate Type (5 classes), Target (5 classes), and Severity (3 classes).
    \item \textbf{Differentiable Soft Consistency Loss:} We formulate a novel loss term that penalizes probabilistic mass assigned to contradictory configurations during backpropagation, reducing logical violations from 9.46\% (336 violations) to 0.03\% (1 violation) while acting as a beneficial regularizer that boosts overall Macro F1.
    \item \textbf{Joint Natural Language Rationales:} We incorporate a lightweight LSTM decoder that generates coherent Bengali natural language explanations alongside classifications, evaluated using the ERASER framework \cite{deyoung2020eraser} to prove explanation faithfulness.
    \item \textbf{Extensive Head-to-Head LLM Benchmarks:} We establish the first comprehensive benchmark comparing specialized small language models (SLMs) against dedicated Bengali LLMs (TigerLLM-1B, TituLLM-1B, BongLLaMA-3B) under both zero-shot and 5-shot paradigms on an identical held-out test set of 3,553 samples.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\subsection{Bengali Hate Speech Detection}
Early automated hate speech detection in Bengali relied on shallow lexical models and support vector machines \cite{romero2019bengali, islam2021bangla}. The release of transformer-based pretrained models, specifically BanglaBERT \cite{bhattacharjee2022banglabert}, established a new standard across Bengali NLP benchmarks by training ELECTRA architectures over large-scale Bengali corpora. More recently, multi-aspect datasets such as BanglaMultiHate \cite{hasan2023banglamultihate} attempted to capture broader dimensions of toxicity. However, existing work treats these annotations as independent targets, overlooking cross-task structural constraints.

\subsection{Multi-Task Learning and Constraint Enforcement}
Multi-Task Learning (MTL) improves generalization by sharing representations across related tasks \cite{caruana1997multitask, liu2019mt}. In NLP, MTL has succeeded in syntactic parsing and sentiment analysis. However, standard hard-parameter sharing does not prevent the output heads from producing mutually exclusive predictions. While post-hoc rule filters can correct contradictions during inference, they do not guide feature representation during training. Our work integrates logical constraints directly into the loss objective via a differentiable penalty.

\subsection{Explainable AI and LLM Benchmarking}
With the deployment of automated moderation systems, explainability (XAI) has become essential for accountability \cite{deyoung2020eraser}. Post-hoc token attribution techniques such as LIME have recently been applied to Bengali comment classification \cite{ashraf2026arrogant}. While useful, attribution heatmaps do not provide grammatical natural language justifications. Meanwhile, evaluating frontier LLMs in multilingual and low-resource environments has revealed stark performance drops compared to English benchmarks \cite{ahuja2023mega}. We bridge these research gaps by evaluating both generative rationales and comparing specialized SLMs directly against Bengali LLMs.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Task Definition and Structural Hierarchy}
Given an input Bengali text sequence $X = (x_1, x_2, \dots, x_L)$, our objective is to predict a tuple $(y^{\text{type}}, y^{\text{target}}, y^{\text{sev}})$ across three taxonomic dimensions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\mathcal{Y}^{\text{type}} = \{\text{None}, \text{Abusive}, \text{Political Hate}, \text{Religious Hate}, \text{Gender Hate}\}$ ($K_1 = 5$)
    \item $\mathcal{Y}^{\text{target}} = \{\text{None}, \text{Individual}, \text{Organization}, \text{Community}, \text{Society}\}$ ($K_2 = 5$)
    \item $\mathcal{Y}^{\text{sev}} = \{\text{Little to None}, \text{Mild}, \text{Severe}\}$ ($K_3 = 3$)
\end{itemize}

The foundational structural dependency governing this taxonomy is:
\begin{equation}
y^{\text{type}} = \text{None} \iff y^{\text{target}} = \text{None} \land y^{\text{sev}} = \text{Little to None}
\label{eq:consistency_rule}
\end{equation}
Any prediction tuple violating Eq. \eqref{eq:consistency_rule} represents a severe logical inconsistency.

\subsection{Model Architecture}
Our architecture consists of three integrated components:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Shared Transformer Encoder:} We utilize pretrained BanglaBERT \cite{bhattacharjee2022banglabert} ($d = 768$, 110M parameters). Given token sequence $X$, the encoder outputs contextual representations:
    \begin{equation}
    \mathbf{H} = \text{BanglaBERT}(X) \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d}
    \end{equation}
    The pooled sequence representation $\mathbf{h}_{\text{CLS}} = \mathbf{H}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^d$ captures global semantic context.
    
    \item \textbf{Multi-Task Classification Heads:} The representation $\mathbf{h}_{\text{CLS}}$ is routed into three parallel two-layer MLPs with ReLU activation and dropout ($p = 0.3$):
    \begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf{y}}^t = \text{Softmax}\left(\mathbf{W}_2^t \text{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}_1^t \mathbf{h}_{\text{CLS}} + \mathbf{b}_1^t) + \mathbf{b}_2^t\right)
    \end{equation}
    for $t \in \{\text{type}, \text{target}, \text{sev}\}$.
    
    \item \textbf{Generative Rationale Decoder:} To provide interpretable explanations, we incorporate an autoregressive LSTM decoder ($d_{\text{embed}} = 256$, $d_{\text{hidden}} = 512$). The initial hidden states $(\mathbf{h}_0, \mathbf{c}_0)$ are projected directly from $\mathbf{h}_{\text{CLS}}$ via learned linear transformations:
    \begin{equation}
    \mathbf{h}_0 = \tanh(\mathbf{W}_h \mathbf{h}_{\text{CLS}}), \quad \mathbf{c}_0 = \tanh(\mathbf{W}_c \mathbf{h}_{\text{CLS}})
    \end{equation}
    The decoder generates explanation tokens $\hat{e}_1, \hat{e}_2, \dots, \hat{e}_M$ conditioned on the encoder's global context.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Loss Formulation}
To mitigate extreme class imbalance (e.g., Gender Hate comprises $<1\%$ of samples), we employ multi-class Focal Loss \cite{lin2017focal} for all classification heads:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{focal}}^t = -\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \alpha_{y_i^t} (1 - P(y_i^t | X_i))^\gamma \log P(y_i^t | X_i)
\end{equation}
where $\gamma = 2.0$ and $\alpha$ is inversely proportional to class frequency.

To strictly penalize violations of Eq. \eqref{eq:consistency_rule} without interrupting gradient flow, we formulate the \textbf{Soft Consistency Loss} $\mathcal{L}_{\text{consist}}$ as:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{consist}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \Big[ &P_i(\text{type}=\text{None}) \cdot \sum_{j \neq \text{None}} P_i(\text{target}=j) \\
&+ P_i(\text{type}=\text{None}) \cdot \sum_{k \neq \text{Little}} P_i(\text{sev}=k) \Big]
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This term is zero if and only if probability mass assigned to $\text{Type} = \text{None}$ is matched by complete concentration on $\text{Target} = \text{None}$ and $\text{Severity} = \text{Little to None}$.

The generative head is trained via standard cross-entropy teacher forcing:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{gen}} = -\frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M \log P(e_m | e_{<m}, \mathbf{h}_{\text{CLS}})
\end{equation}

The unified joint training objective is:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{focal}}^{\text{type}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{focal}}^{\text{target}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{focal}}^{\text{sev}} + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{consist}} + \delta \mathcal{L}_{\text{gen}}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda = 1.0$ and $\delta = 0.5$.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Dataset and Stratified Partitioning}
We evaluate on a curated corpus of 35,530 Bengali social media comments. Following linguistic validation, we consolidated overlapping categories from the original BanglaMultiHate taxonomy \cite{hasan2023banglamultihate} (merging `Profane' into `Abusive' and refining `Sexism' into `Gender Hate').

To prevent any data contamination or leakage, the corpus is partitioned deterministically using a stratified 80/10/10 split ($\text{seed} = 42$) based on the Hate Type distribution:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Training Set (80\%):} 28,424 samples (used exclusively for parameter updates in Notebook 03).
    \item \textbf{Validation Set (10\%):} 3,553 samples (used exclusively for early stopping and checkpoint selection).
    \item \textbf{Test Set (10\%):} 3,553 samples (strictly held-out benchmark evaluated in Notebooks 04 and 05a--d).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Baseline Models}
We evaluate our system against two distinct paradigms:
\subsubsection{Ablation Variants (Our Architecture, 110M)}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{exp1 (Baseline MTL):} BanglaBERT + 3 classification heads ($\lambda = 0, \delta = 0$).
    \item \textbf{exp2 (+Consistency Loss):} Baseline MTL trained with $\mathcal{L}_{\text{consist}}$ ($\lambda = 1.0, \delta = 0$).
    \item \textbf{exp3 (+Generative Head):} Baseline MTL trained with LSTM explanation decoder ($\lambda = 0, \delta = 0.5$).
    \item \textbf{exp4 (Full Model):} Complete architecture with both consistency loss and generative head ($\lambda = 1.0, \delta = 0.5$).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Open-Source Bengali Large Language Models}
We benchmark three prominent open-source Bengali LLMs under both Zero-Shot and 5-Shot in-context learning:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{TigerLLM-1B-it} \cite{nishat2025tigerllm}: SOTA instruction-tuned Bengali LLM (1B parameters, ACL 2025).
    \item \textbf{TituLLM-1B} \cite{hishab2024titullm}: Foundation Bengali LLM developed by Hishab (1B parameters).
    \item \textbf{BongLLaMA-3B-Instruct} \cite{banglallm2024bongllama}: The largest publicly available Bengali instruction model (3B parameters).
\end{itemize}
All LLMs are prompted with explicit taxonomy guidelines and instructed to respond strictly in structured JSON. The 5-shot demonstrations are sampled exclusively from the training set to prevent test contamination. A robust multi-tier parser extracts labels via regular expressions if JSON formatting fails, ensuring fair evaluation.

\subsection{Comparative Benchmarking with Bengali LLMs}
Table \ref{tab:llm_benchmark} presents the comprehensive head-to-head evaluation between our specialized 110M model and open-source Bengali LLMs on the identical 3,553 test samples. Key insights include:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Significant Accuracy Superiority (+68.8\%):} Our 110M model outperforms the strongest LLM baseline (TigerLLM 5-shot) by +68.8\% relative Macro F1 (0.5576 vs. 0.3303). The LLMs struggle acutely on fine-grained minority classes (e.g., Gender Hate and Religious Hate).
    \item \textbf{Inference Latency \& Deployment Efficiency:} Our model processes each comment in **0.0152 seconds** ($\approx 66$ comments/second on a single T4 GPU). In contrast, TigerLLM requires **5.85 seconds/sample** ($\approx 390\times$ slower). For social media streams processing millions of daily comments, LLMs are computationally untenable.
    \item \textbf{Output Determinism \& Parse Rates:} While our classification heads guarantee 100\% valid structured outputs, LLMs exhibit severe parsing fragility. Zero-shot BongLLaMA achieved a 0\% JSON parse rate, while TituLLM parsed only 5.7\% of zero-shot responses and 21.5\% of 5-shot responses.
    \item \textbf{Mode Collapse and Semantic Inconsistency:} Unconstrained generative models frequently break consistency rules. In 5-shot mode, TigerLLM committed 47 violations (1.32\% CVR), TituLLM committed 177 violations (4.98\% CVR), and BongLLaMA suffered severe degenerate collapse (100\% CVR, labeling every comment as Type=None while assigning non-None targets and severities).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
We presented a Consistency-Constrained Multi-Task Learning framework for multi-aspect Bengali hate speech detection. By introducing a differentiable Soft Consistency Loss, our model eliminates 99.7\% of logical contradictions on held-out test data while boosting Average Macro F1 to 0.558. Extensive benchmarking demonstrates that our 110M specialized model outperforms 1B--3B Bengali LLMs by +68.8\% in Macro F1 while running $\sim$390$\times$ faster with 100\% deterministic parsing. Furthermore, integrated explanation decoding yields faithful natural language rationales validated via ERASER metrics. These findings prove that task-specific structural inductive biases are far more effective, reliable, and cost-efficient than raw parameter scale for structured content moderation in low-resource languages.
